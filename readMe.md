1. INTRODUCTION

Credit cards are world-wide used and a great number of transaction are totally legal and 
regular. Unfortunately, this payment system may be used to perpetrate frauds and steal money.
Due to customers' claims it is possible to classify a credit card transaction as regular or fraudulent. 
Based on a dataset downloaded from kaggle.com
(https://www.kaggle.com/datasets/chetanmittal033credit-card-fraud-data), I want to 
better understand this events.


1.1. Project goal

The project's goal is to analyse a dataset that contains quite a robust series of  this kind of transactions and asses whether they are fraud or not.
After that, another purpose is to investigate how and how well it is possibile to use a machine learning approach to detect if a transaction is (or may possibly be) a fraud or not.


1.2. Problem definition and challenges

The idea is to dive deep into the dataset and understand which data (variables) are more bound to be a "fraud-marker".
In a typical binary classfication problem, the big issue is that, by its nature, the starting point is made of a massively unbalanced dataset where (luckily) very little cases are positive (fraud) and the vast majority refers to negative (regular transactions).


1.3. Contextual background

Why is this a topic such interesting?
Because a lot of people use this payment system in an increasing variety of situations: physical shops, on-line shops, bills direct debt, and so on.
Credit card are used to pay almost every kind of purchase in a quite relevant price range (let's roughly say from 1 to 1000 euros/dollars at least) which covers the most of
nearly every customer.
Again, they are used to pay so many different products (from food to clothes, from travels to consumer electronics).
Interpreting the final reason behind a card transaction is crucial to make each customer safe and to reinforce the reputation of this means of payment which, amongst the others, was created to allow people to avoid carry too much cash with themselves.
